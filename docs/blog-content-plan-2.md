The ten requested keywords were checked against the live site (pages, H1s and the 10 existing blog posts listed in [blog-keyword-plan.md](blog-keyword-plan.md)).

| 4 | is apollo tv illegal | `/is-apollo-group-tv-legal` (same intent) | **Optimize existing page** + replacement article |
| 5 | what is apollo group tv | `/` and `/product` (brand definition) | **Optimize existing page** + replacement article |
| 6 | how much is apollo tv | `/plans` (pricing) | **Optimize existing page** + replacement article |
| 7 | how to download apollo group tv on android | `/guides/android-phone-tablet`, `/guides/fire-tv-android-tv` | **Optimize existing page** + replacement article |
| 8 | how to download apollo group tv on downloader | partly `/guides/fire-tv-android-tv` | **New article**, scoped to the Downloader app itself |
| 9 | how to download apollo group tv on fire tv | `/guides/fire-tv-android-tv` (same intent) | **Optimize existing page** + replacement article |
| 10 | how to install apollo tv | `/guides` hub | **Optimize existing page** + replacement article |

**Why not write all ten anyway?** Keywords 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10 have the same search intent as pages that already exist. A blog post on the same intent splits ranking signals between two URLs, and Google usually shows only one of them. The stronger move is to optimize the page that already owns the intent and use the blog slot for a keyword nobody on the site targets yet.

Keywords 7, 9 and 10 also overlap **each other** ("download on Android", "download on Fire TV", "install"). Even on brand-new pages they would compete, which is another reason to map them to the existing device guides.

**Result:** 4 new articles on the requested keywords + 6 replacement articles on new keywords = **10 new articles**, plus 6 page optimizations.
