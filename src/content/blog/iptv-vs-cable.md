For decades, live TV meant cable or satellite: a box from the provider, a monthly bill and usually a contract. IPTV delivers the same kind of live channels over your internet connection instead, on a device you own. For many households that's a better deal; for some, cable still makes sense.

This guide compares the two honestly. It covers how each one delivers TV, what you actually pay for (including the costs that hide on cable bills), reliability, picture quality and equipment, and it ends with a checklist for switching without surprises. We don't quote prices here, because they change and vary by area; instead, we show you what to compare on your own bills.

## How each one delivers TV

### Cable

Cable TV arrives over a dedicated network that your provider runs into your home. The provider's set-top box tunes and decodes the channels. Because the TV signal has its own capacity on that network, it isn't affected by how busy your home internet is.

### IPTV

IPTV arrives over your internet connection, the same one you use for everything else. A device you choose (a streaming stick, an Android TV box, a computer or a phone) requests each channel from the provider's servers and plays it. Our guide to [what IPTV is and how it works](/blog/what-is-iptv) explains the process step by step.

The key consequence: with IPTV, the quality of your home internet and Wi-Fi becomes part of your TV experience.

## What you pay for

Comparing prices fairly means looking at the whole bill, not just the advertised monthly figure.

### Costs to look for on a cable bill

Cable bills often include more than the package price. Depending on your provider and area, you may see:

- **Equipment rental** for each set-top box, often charged per TV.
- **Additional box fees** for every extra room.
- **Broadcast or regional fees**, sometimes listed separately from the package price.
- **Promotional pricing** that rises after an introductory period.
- **Contract terms**, with fees for ending early.
- **Installation or activation charges.**

Pull out a recent bill and add up every line, not just the headline package price. That total is the figure to compare.

### Costs with IPTV

With IPTV, you pay for:

- **The subscription itself.** With Apollo Group TV, every pack is a one-time payment for a fixed period with no automatic renewal. Current prices are on the [Plans page](/plans).
- **A streaming device per TV**, if you don't already have one. This is a one-off purchase you own, rather than a monthly rental.
- **Your internet connection**, which you probably already pay for. Check whether your plan has enough speed and headroom for live TV.

### The bundle question

Many households buy TV and internet together. When you drop cable TV, the price of internet on its own can change, sometimes going up because the bundle discount disappears. Ask your provider what the internet-only price would be before you cancel. That number belongs in your comparison.

## Reliability and what affects it

### Cable's strength

Cable's main advantage is consistency. The TV signal doesn't compete with your household's internet use, and it isn't affected by Wi-Fi coverage in your home. When it fails, it's usually an outage in your area.

### What affects IPTV reliability

With IPTV, reliability depends on several links in a chain:

- **Your internet connection**, especially at busy evening times.
- **Your home Wi-Fi**, particularly in rooms far from the router.
- **Your streaming device**, which needs enough storage and memory.
- **The provider's service.**

The first three are in your control. A wired connection or a mesh Wi-Fi node near the TV removes most Wi-Fi problems; our comparison of [Ethernet and Wi-Fi on a Firestick](/blog/firestick-ethernet-vs-wifi) explains how. Keeping the device tidy, as described in our [Firestick cache guide](/blog/clear-cache-firestick), keeps it responsive.

### A realistic expectation

On a steady, well-sized connection with a good device, IPTV feels much like cable. On weak Wi-Fi in a far bedroom, it won't, until the Wi-Fi is fixed.

## Picture quality and 4K

Both cable and IPTV can deliver HD, and both can deliver 4K where the channel is broadcast in 4K. In practice:

- **Many live channels are broadcast in HD**, whichever service you use.
- **4K needs a 4K TV, a 4K-capable device and more bandwidth.** For IPTV, published guidelines suggest 15 to 25 Mbps per 4K stream, and live TV benefits from extra headroom. See our guide to the [internet speed you need for IPTV](/blog/internet-speed-for-iptv).
- **Picture quality on IPTV can adapt** to your connection. If bandwidth drops, some players lower the quality briefly rather than stopping.

## Recording and catch-up

Many people rely on a cable DVR to record programmes. With IPTV, recording and catch-up depend on the service and the player app you use; they aren't automatically included. If recording matters to you, check what the service and your chosen player support before you cancel cable, and don't assume a feature exists because another IPTV service offers it.

## Data caps and your internet plan

Cable TV doesn't use your internet allowance. IPTV does. If your internet plan has a monthly data cap, live TV can use a large share of it: an hour of HD uses roughly 2 GB at 5 Mbps, and 4K uses several times more. Our guide to the [internet speed you need for IPTV](/blog/internet-speed-for-iptv) includes a table for estimating monthly usage. Check your plan's cap before switching, and consider an unlimited plan if your household watches a lot of live TV.

## Live sport and big events

Live sport is where the difference between cable and IPTV is most noticeable, because it's live, fast-moving and watched by many people at once.

- **A steady connection matters most.** Sport has a high bitrate and no buffer to hide behind. A wired connection or strong 5 GHz Wi-Fi gives you the best margin.
- **Close other heavy uses** during big matches, such as large downloads or game updates.
- **Expect a small delay.** Internet streams usually run a little behind broadcast TV. If a neighbour's cheer arrives before the goal on your screen, that's why.

## Flexibility and moving home

IPTV has practical advantages when circumstances change:

- **Moving house** means taking your streaming device with you and connecting it to the new internet connection, with no installation appointment for TV.
- **Watching in another room** is as simple as moving the stick or using a phone or tablet.
- **No long contracts.** With one-time packs, you decide whether to continue when a pack ends.

## Who should stick with cable?

IPTV isn't the best choice for everyone. Cable may still suit you if:

- Your internet connection is slow, unreliable or has a low data cap.
- You can't improve Wi-Fi in the rooms where you watch, and can't run cables.
- You rely on a specific cable feature you'd miss, such as a particular recording setup.
- Your bundle makes internet much more expensive without TV.

## Who's a good fit for IPTV?

IPTV tends to suit households that:

- Have a steady broadband connection with headroom.
- Want to avoid contracts and monthly box rentals.
- Already own streaming devices or are happy to buy one per TV.
- Watch on phones, tablets and computers as well as TVs.

## A checklist before you cancel cable

Switching is easiest if you overlap the two for a short time. Work through this list:

1. **Check your contract.** Note your end date and any early-termination fee.
2. **Ask for the internet-only price.** Get it in writing if you can.
3. **Test your connection on the TV device** in the evening, using our speed guide.
4. **Set up IPTV first.** Choose a shorter pack, install the app on your main TV and watch for a few evenings while you still have cable.
5. **Fix any Wi-Fi weak spots** you discover during the overlap.
6. **Check your connection limit** covers the number of TVs your household uses at once. Our article on [IPTV connection limits](/blog/iptv-connection-limit) explains how streams are counted.
7. **Cancel cable**, and keep a record of the cancellation and any reference number.
8. **Return rented equipment** promptly. Unreturned boxes are a common source of extra charges.

Here's how a household might run the numbers, without quoting any real prices.

They list every line on their cable bill: the package, two box rentals, a regional fee and a promotional discount that ends next month. That gives their true monthly cable cost. They ask their provider for the internet-only price, which is a little higher than the internet portion of their bundle.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is IPTV cheaper than cable?

It often is once you include box rentals and fees on a cable bill, but it depends on your area, your bundle and your internet price after cancelling TV. Compare your full cable bill with internet-only plus your IPTV subscription and devices.

### Is IPTV as reliable as cable?

On a steady connection with good Wi-Fi or a wired connection, it can feel just as reliable. IPTV depends on your home internet, so weak Wi-Fi or a congested connection shows up as buffering.

### Can I keep my TV when switching from cable to IPTV?

Yes. Any TV with an HDMI port works with a streaming stick or box.

### Do I need fast internet for IPTV?

You need a steady connection with headroom: around 5 Mbps per HD stream and 15 to 25 Mbps per 4K stream as a guideline, plus extra for live TV and the rest of your household.

### Will I lose local channels if I cancel cable?

It depends on what your IPTV service includes and where you live. Many areas also receive local channels free over the air with an antenna. Check before you cancel.

### What happens to my cable box when I cancel?

Rented boxes usually have to be returned to the cable provider. Check their instructions and keep proof of return, because unreturned equipment is a common source of extra charges.

### Can I watch IPTV on my phone as well as the TV?

Yes. With Apollo Group TV you can watch on phones, tablets and computers as well as TVs, within your subscription's connection limit.

### Can I try IPTV before cancelling cable?

Yes, and it's the best approach. Choose a shorter pack and run both for a few evenings to make sure your connection and devices are ready.

## Conclusion

Cable's strength is consistency independent of your home internet. IPTV's strengths are flexibility, no box rentals and no contracts, at the cost of depending on your connection and Wi-Fi. The fair comparison is your full cable bill against internet-only plus an IPTV subscription and any devices you need to buy. Overlap the two for a few evenings before cancelling, and you'll know for certain which works better in your home.
