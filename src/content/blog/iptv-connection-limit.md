Every IPTV subscription comes with a connection limit, and it causes more confusion than almost anything else. People install the app on four devices, see an error on the third, and assume something is broken. Others share a login with family and wonder why streams keep stopping in the evening.

Most of the confusion comes from one misunderstanding: a connection limit usually counts **streams playing at the same time**, not the number of devices you've installed the app on. Below: what's counted, why limits exist, what happens when you go over, and how to plan for a home with several TVs.

## What a connection limit counts

Think of your connection limit as the number of video streams your login can play at the same moment.

- **Installed devices don't count on their own.** The app on a TV that's switched off isn't using a connection.
- **Active streams count.** Each device playing a channel or video at the same time uses one connection.
- **Paused or backgrounded streams may still count.** Some players keep a stream open briefly after you pause or switch apps. Closing the app properly frees the connection faster.

So if your limit is 2, you could install the app on a Fire TV, a phone, a tablet and a laptop, and watch on any two of them at the same time.

## Why IPTV services have connection limits

Limits aren't unique to IPTV. Most streaming subscriptions restrict how many screens can watch at once. The reasons are practical:

- **Capacity.** Each stream uses server capacity and bandwidth on the provider's side. Limits keep the service steady for everyone.
- **Fair pricing.** A subscription priced for one household isn't priced for five households sharing it.
- **Account security.** If someone else gets hold of your login, a limit restricts how much they can use it, and an unexpected "limit reached" message can be your first sign.

## What happens when you go over the limit

When a device tries to start a stream and all your connections are already in use, the result depends on the player app. You might see:

- A message such as "maximum connections reached" or "connection limit exceeded".
- The new stream failing to start, or starting and stopping after a few seconds.
- An existing stream on another device stopping.

None of these mean the service is down. They mean every connection is busy.

## How to fix "maximum connections reached"

Work through these steps in order.

### 1. Count what's actually playing

Walk around the house. Is a TV in the bedroom still on a channel? Is a phone in someone's pocket playing in the background? A forgotten stream is the most common cause.

### 2. Close the app properly

On Fire TV and Android TV, pressing Home doesn't always close the app; it may keep running. To stop it fully on Fire TV, go to **Settings > Applications > Manage Installed Applications**, select the app and choose **Force stop**. On phones, close the app from the recent-apps screen.

### 3. Wait a minute

Servers sometimes take a short time to notice a stream has ended, especially if a device lost power or Wi-Fi abruptly. Waiting a minute or two before retrying often clears it.

### 4. Restart the device you're trying to watch on

A restart closes any half-open connection from that device.

### 5. Check for unexpected use

If the error appears when nobody else in your home is watching, someone outside your household may be using your login. [Contact our support team](/contact) and ask about changing your player login details. Never post your login details publicly or share them in groups.

## Planning connections for a household

The right connection limit depends on how many people watch at the same time at your busiest moment, not how many screens you own.

### Step 1: Picture your busiest evening

Who's watching what, and where? Count the streams that genuinely overlap. A typical example:

| Time | Living room TV | Bedroom TV | Phone | Streams at once |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 pm | Live sport | — | — | 1 |
| 8 pm | Live sport | Film | — | 2 |
| 9 pm | Film | Film | Highlights | 3 |

This household peaks at three streams for an hour, so a limit of two would cause problems at 9 pm.

### Step 2: Check your internet can handle it

More connections only help if your internet speed supports them. Several HD and 4K streams at once need real headroom; the FCC's [Household Broadband Guide](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/household-broadband-guide) shows how quickly multiple devices add up. Our guide to the [internet speed you need for IPTV](/blog/internet-speed-for-iptv) shows how to add it up.

### Step 3: Remember Wi-Fi is shared too

Even with enough connections and enough speed, several streams on a weak Wi-Fi network can buffer. TVs that are far from the router benefit from a wired connection; see our comparison of [Ethernet and Wi-Fi on a Firestick](/blog/firestick-ethernet-vs-wifi).

## Common questions about sharing

### Can family members use the same login?

Within your household, using your login on several of your own devices is what connection limits are designed for. Sharing a login with people in other homes is a different matter: it uses your connections, can trigger limit errors at the worst moments, and makes it harder to keep your account secure. Our [Terms and Conditions](/terms-and-conditions) explain the rules for your account.

### Can I watch on the same device in two apps?

If two apps on one device each play a stream, they use two connections. The same applies to picture-in-picture or multi-view features in some players.

### Does watching in a browser count?

Yes. Any device or app playing a stream with your login uses a connection while it plays.

## How connections are counted in everyday situations

The basic rule (one playing stream, one connection) covers most cases, but a few everyday situations catch people out. Here's how they usually work:

| Situation | Connections used |
|---|---|
| One TV playing a live channel | 1 |
| Two TVs playing different channels | 2 |
| Two TVs playing the same channel | 2 (each device has its own stream) |
| TV playing, phone app open on the channel list but not playing | Usually 1 |
| Phone playing with the screen locked | 1, if the app keeps playing in the background |
| Laptop playing your M3U playlist in VLC while the TV plays | 2 |
| A device that lost Wi-Fi mid-stream | May count for a short while until the server notices |

Two TVs watching the same channel surprises people most. Each device opens its own stream from the server, so each uses a connection, even if the picture is identical.

### Multi-view and picture-in-picture

Some players can show two or four channels on one screen at once. Each tile is a separate stream, so a four-way multi-view uses four connections. If you like watching several games at once, plan your limit around it.

## Watching while you travel

Your subscription goes where your devices go. Watching on a phone or laptop away from home uses a connection exactly as it would at home, so if someone is also watching on the living room TV, that's two connections.

A few practical points for travelling:

- **Hotel and public Wi-Fi** is often slow or limited, and some networks restrict streaming. Your mobile data may be steadier; check your allowance first.
- **Your login works the same way everywhere** your devices have internet, but channel availability is based on the licensing behind each channel, and using a VPN to get around regional licensing isn't allowed under our terms. Our page on [whether Apollo Group TV is legal](/is-apollo-group-tv-legal) explains why licensing works this way.
- **Sign in before you leave.** Setting up a new player on hotel Wi-Fi with a phone keyboard is no fun. Install and test the app at home.

## Signs someone else may be using your login

Connection errors with nobody else watching at home are the clearest sign. Others to watch for:

- Errors at odd times of day when your household isn't watching.
- Your login was posted, shared in a group chat, or entered on a device you no longer own.
- A previous household member or guest still has the app set up with your details.

If you see any of these, change what you can control first: remove the app or sign out on devices you no longer use, and stop sharing the details. Then [contact our support team](/contact) and ask whether your player login can be reset. After a reset, update the details on each of your own devices from My Account.

## Example: solving evening errors without changing plans

Here's a common pattern. A household with a connection limit of 2 reports that the bedroom TV shows a connection error most evenings, even though "only two people are watching".

Looking more closely:

- The living room TV is on a live channel.
- The bedroom TV tries to start a film.
- A teenager's phone is still playing a channel in the background, muted, from earlier in the evening.

Three streams were open. Closing the app on the phone properly fixed it. The household then agreed a simple habit: close the app when you stop watching, rather than just locking the phone. If the household genuinely needed three streams at once, the right fix would be a pack with a higher limit rather than workarounds.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does installing the app on more devices use up my connections?

No. Connections are used by streams that are playing. You can install the app on more devices than your limit and use any of them, as long as no more than your limit play at once.

### Why do I get a connection error when only one person is watching?

Usually a stream is still open somewhere: a TV left on, an app running in the background, or a device that lost Wi-Fi without closing the stream. Close apps fully, wait a minute and try again. If it continues, contact support.

### How do I see my connection limit?

Open My Account in your dashboard once your subscription is active. The connection limit is shown with your expiry date.

### Can I increase my connection limit?

Limits are set per subscription. If your household regularly needs more streams at once, ask our support team about the options when you next choose a pack.

### Does a paused stream count as a connection?

It can. Some players keep the stream open while paused or in the background. Closing the app releases it.

### Is it safe to share my login with friends?

We don't recommend it. It uses your connections, can cause errors for you, and increases the risk of your login spreading further. Keep your details private.

## Conclusion

A connection limit counts streams playing at the same time, not the devices you've installed on. When you see a "maximum connections" message, something is usually still playing somewhere, and closing apps properly fixes most cases. For a busy household, plan around your busiest evening, check that your internet speed and Wi-Fi can carry that many streams, and pick a pack whose limit fits.

Your current limit is always in [My Account](/dashboard/subscription). If anything doesn't add up, [our support team](/contact) can check your subscription for you.
