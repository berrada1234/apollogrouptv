Getting Apollo TV isn't complicated, but it does involve a few separate pieces: a device that can run the app, an internet connection that can carry live TV, an account, a pack, a payment and an installation. Done in the right order, it takes well under an hour. Done in the wrong order, you can end up paying before discovering your TV stick can't install the app.

This guide is the map. It walks through the whole journey in the order that avoids surprises, explains what each step involves, and points you to the detailed guide for each one. If you just want to start, the five steps are summarized right below.

## The five steps at a glance

1. **Check your device** can run the Apollo TV app.
2. **Check your internet** is ready for live TV.
3. **Create your account and choose a pack.**
4. **Pay and wait for activation.**
5. **Install the app and sign in** with your login details.

The first two steps take five minutes and save most of the problems people run into later.

## What you need before you start

| You need | Why | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| A supported device | The app has to run on it | Step 1 below |
| A steady internet connection | Live TV needs consistency | Step 2 below |
| An email address | For your account | Any email you can access |
| A payment method | To pay for your pack | See the [payment methods](/payment-methods) |
| About 30 to 60 minutes | For the first setup | Most of it is waiting for downloads and activation |

You don't need a contract, a cable box or a satellite dish. Every pack is a one-time payment for a fixed period, and nothing renews automatically.

## Step 1: Check your device is supported

The Apollo TV app runs on a range of devices, and the list of verified devices is on our [Apollo TV app page](/app). At the time of writing, it covers:

- **Fire TV and Android TV devices**, installed with the free Downloader app.
- **Android phones and tablets**, installed from an official download.
- **Windows and Mac computers.**
- **iPhone, iPad and Apple TV**, using a compatible player app with your login details.

### A warning about new Fire TV models

Some Fire TV models released from late 2025 onwards run a new system called Vega OS instead of the Android-based Fire OS. Vega OS devices only install apps from the Amazon Appstore, so they can't install the Apollo TV app. If you're buying a Fire TV stick for Apollo TV, read our guide to the [best Fire TV Stick for IPTV](/blog/best-firestick-for-iptv) first; it explains how to check which system a device runs.

### Don't have a supported device yet?

A streaming stick is usually the cheapest way to watch on a TV. If you already own a Samsung or LG smart TV, our article on [watching Apollo TV on a Samsung or LG TV](/blog/apollo-tv-samsung-lg-smart-tv) explains your options.

## Step 2: Make sure your internet is ready

Live TV is more demanding than films or series on demand, because it can only buffer a few seconds ahead. Speed matters, but consistency matters more.

### Quick checks

- **Run a speed test on the device you'll watch on**, not your phone next to the router.
- **Test in the evening**, when connections are busiest.
- **Look at how much the result varies**, not just the top number.

Published guidelines suggest around 5 Mbps for one HD stream and 15 to 25 Mbps for one 4K stream. For live TV, aim for roughly double that per stream your household watches at once. Our guide to the [internet speed you need for IPTV](/blog/internet-speed-for-iptv) shows how to add it up for your home.

If your TV is far from the router, a wired connection or a mesh Wi-Fi node near the TV makes a big difference. See [Ethernet vs Wi-Fi on a Firestick](/blog/firestick-ethernet-vs-wifi).

## Step 3: Create your account and choose a pack

### Create your account

Go to [create an account](/create-account), enter your name, email and a password, and accept the terms. Depending on how the site is set up, you may be asked to verify your email by clicking a link. Our [account setup guide](/guides/account-setup) covers this step by step.

### Choose your pack

Packs differ by length: the longer the pack, the lower the cost per month. Compare them on the [Plans page](/plans), which shows the current price, the length and the cost per month for each.

Things to consider:

- **How long do you want access?** Longer packs cost less per month but are paid upfront.
- **How many screens do you watch at once?** Each subscription has a connection limit, explained in our article on [IPTV connection limits](/blog/iptv-connection-limit).
- **Are you new to IPTV?** A shorter pack lets you try it on your own devices first.

If you choose a pack on the Plans page before signing up, it's kept for you while you create your account, and your invoice is waiting when you arrive.

## Step 4: Pay and wait for activation

### Your invoice

When you choose a pack, an invoice appears at the top of Plans & Billing with its own invoice number and a reservation timer. The timer holds your price while you pay.

### Paying

Press **Continue Payment**, pick a payment method and follow the instructions in the payment window. Each method has its own step-by-step guide in our [payment guides](/payment-guides), from Cash App and Venmo to MoonPay and crypto wallets.

When you've sent the payment, press **I Have Paid**. This lets our team know to check it, and takes you to a page that shows your payment's status.

### Activation

Once your payment is confirmed, our team activates your subscription and adds your IPTV login details to your account. You'll get a notification in your dashboard when it's ready. How payment confirmation works for each method is explained in [how checkout works](/payment-guides/how-checkout-works).

## Step 5: Install the app and sign in

### Install

Follow the [setup guide for your device](/guides). For Fire TV and Android TV, you'll install the free Downloader app and use the official code; our article on the [Apollo TV Downloader code](/blog/apollo-tv-downloader-code) covers that tool in detail.

### Find your login details

Open [My Account](/dashboard/subscription). Once your subscription is active, it shows everything you need: your username and password, playlist links, Xtream login details for player apps, your expiry date and your connection limit. Each item has a copy button.

### Sign in and start watching

Open the app, enter your details, and give it a minute to load the channel list the first time.

## Picking a device for each TV

If you're setting up more than one screen, it helps to plan which device goes where:

| Screen | Good option | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Main TV with a 4K picture | A Fire OS Fire TV Stick 4K Max or a Fire TV Cube | More storage and better Wi-Fi; the Cube has a network port |
| Second TV in HD | A Fire OS Fire TV Stick or an Android TV box | Enough for HD, lower cost |
| Phone or tablet | The Android app, or a player on iPhone and iPad | Watch anywhere at home |
| Laptop or desktop | The Windows or Mac app | Handy for a desk or kitchen |

Remember that each screen playing at the same time uses one of your subscription's connections, so check your connection limit before planning several TVs.

## Mistakes to avoid

These are the slip-ups that most often slow new customers down:

- **Buying a Vega OS Fire TV stick.** Check the operating system before you buy; Vega OS models can't install the Apollo TV app.
- **Using your website password in the app.** Your IPTV login details are different from your website login. They're in My Account.
- **Paying outside your account.** Only pay through the payment window linked to your own invoice. Anyone asking you to pay elsewhere isn't us; our guide to the [official Apollo Group TV website](/blog/apollo-group-tv-official-website) explains how to spot lookalike sites.
- **Sending the wrong amount.** For Bitcoin-based methods, send the exact amount shown, and remember that some apps take their fee out of what you send. Our article on [Bitcoin network fees](/payment-guides/bitcoin-network-fees) explains how to avoid a short payment.
- **Skipping the internet check.** A connection that's fine for films can still struggle with live TV in the evening.

## What happens when your pack ends

Your expiry date is shown in My Account. A few days before it, you'll get a reminder notification in your dashboard. When the pack ends, channels stop loading until you choose another pack. If you buy a new pack while your current one is still running, the new period is normally set to begin when the current one ends, so you don't lose any days. Our [Apollo Group TV renewal guide](/apollo-group-tv-renewal) walks through renewing step by step.

## After you're set up

A few things worth knowing in your first week:

- **Keep the app updated.** On Fire TV, see [how to update Apollo TV on a Firestick](/blog/update-apollo-tv-firestick); for other devices, [how to update Apollo Group TV](/blog/how-to-update-apollo-group-tv).
- **Bookmark My Account.** It's always where your current login details and expiry date live.
- **Turn on subtitles if you want them.** Our guide to [getting subtitles on Apollo Group TV](/blog/apollo-group-tv-subtitles) shows where the setting is on each device.
- **Know where to get help.** The [Help Center](/help) brings together every guide and answer, and you can open a support ticket from your dashboard any time.

## Example: a first-time setup, start to finish

Here's how a typical first setup goes for someone with a Fire TV Stick and Wi-Fi.

- They check the stick under **Settings > My Fire TV > About** and confirm it runs Fire OS.
- A speed test on the stick in the evening shows a steady 40 Mbps, comfortable for HD.
- They choose a pack on the Plans page, create an account, and land on their invoice.
- They pay with one of the payment methods, following its guide, and press I Have Paid.
- While waiting for activation, they install Downloader and the Apollo TV app.
- When the activation notification arrives, they copy their login from My Account into the app and start watching.

Doing the installation while waiting for activation is the time-saver: by the time the login details arrive, everything else is ready.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I sign up for Apollo TV?

Create an account on our website, choose a pack on the Plans page and pay your invoice. Your login details appear in My Account once your subscription is activated.

### What devices can I use Apollo TV on?

Fire TV and Android TV devices, Android phones and tablets, Windows and Mac computers, and iPhone, iPad or Apple TV with a compatible player. The current list is on our app page.

### How long does activation take after I pay?

Your payment is checked first, then our team activates your subscription and adds your login details. You'll get a notification in your dashboard when it's ready.

### Do I need a credit card to get Apollo TV?

No. You can pay with several methods, including Cash App, Venmo, MoonPay and crypto wallets. Our payment guides explain each one.

### Does Apollo TV renew automatically?

No. Every pack is a one-time payment for a fixed period. When it ends, you choose whether to buy another.

### What if my payment hasn't been confirmed yet?

After you press I Have Paid, your invoice shows Pending Confirmation while the payment is checked. You'll get a notification when it's approved. If something looks wrong, our payment troubleshooting guide covers the common cases.

### Can I change my pack after paying?

Once a pack is paid and activated, it runs for its full length. You can buy another pack at any time, and it normally starts when your current one ends. For anything else, contact our support team and see the refund policy.

### Can I get Apollo TV on a Samsung or LG smart TV?

Not directly with the Apollo TV app, but you can plug a supported streaming stick into the TV's HDMI port. Our article on Samsung and LG TVs explains the options.

### Can I use Apollo TV on more than one TV?

Yes, within your subscription's connection limit, which is shown in My Account.

Getting Apollo TV comes down to five steps: check your device, check your internet, create an account and choose a pack, pay, then install and sign in. Doing the two quick checks first is what makes the rest go smoothly, especially with newer Fire TV models that can't install the app.
