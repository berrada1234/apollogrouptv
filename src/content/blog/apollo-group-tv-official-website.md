## Warning signs of a fake or lookalike site

Lookalike sites tend to share the same patterns. The FTC's guide on [how to avoid a scam](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-scam) lists several of them in general terms, and they map closely onto fake streaming sites.

### A slightly different web address

The name looks right, but the domain doesn't: an extra letter, a hyphen, a swapped character, or a different ending such as .net instead of .tv. Registering misspelled versions of well-known names is common enough to have a name: [typosquatting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typosquatting). Always check the full address in your browser's address bar.

### Payment outside an account

A real service lets you pay inside your account and gives you an invoice. Be very cautious if a site or a person asks you to:

- Pay by gift card.
- Send money to a personal payment-app account you were messaged about.
- Send cryptocurrency to an address given in a chat, email or social media message.
- Pay "a small fee" to unlock or fix an existing subscription.

On our site, payment details only appear inside your own payment window, tied to your invoice number.

### Pressure and urgency

"Only 2 spots left", "offer ends in 10 minutes", "your account will be deleted today unless you pay". Pressure is designed to stop you from checking. A genuine service is still there tomorrow.

### Offers that don't add up

Lifetime access for the price of a month, unlimited connections for one payment, or prices far below everyone else's. If an offer seems too good, it usually is.

### Contact only through messaging apps

If the only way to reach a "provider" is a messaging app, with no website account, published policies or support system, you have very little recourse if something goes wrong.

### Login details sent by email or message

We show your IPTV login only in My Account. If you receive login details by email or message claiming to be from Apollo Group TV, treat them with suspicion and check My Account instead.

## Checks you can run on any website before paying

These take a few minutes and work for any online purchase.

### 1. Type the address yourself

Rather than clicking links in ads, messages or emails, type the address into your browser or use a bookmark you saved yourself. Ads and links can point to lookalike sites.

### 2. Read the address carefully

Look at the whole domain, especially the part just before the ending (.tv, .com and so on). Check each letter.

### 3. Don't rely on the padlock alone

The padlock icon means your connection to the site is encrypted. It doesn't mean the site is honest; scam sites can have padlocks too. It's necessary but not sufficient.

### 4. Check the site's safety status

Google's [Safe Browsing site status tool](https://transparencyreport.google.com/safe-browsing/search) lets you enter an address and see whether Google currently flags it as unsafe. A clean result isn't a guarantee, but a warning is a clear stop sign.

### 5. Look up how old the domain is

ICANN's [registration data lookup](https://lookup.icann.org/) shows public registration details for a domain, including when it was created. A domain registered a few weeks ago that claims years of history is a red flag. Many registrations hide owner details for privacy, which is normal; the creation date is the useful part.

### 6. Read the policies

A real service has terms, a privacy policy and a refund policy that say something specific. Copied or empty policies, or none at all, are a warning sign.

### 7. Be wary of "apps" offered by a site you don't trust

Fake sites sometimes offer their own app download or a Downloader code. Only install the Apollo TV app with the official code from our [setup guides](/guides), and keep it current using our guide to [updating Apollo TV on a Firestick](/blog/update-apollo-tv-firestick). An app from an untrusted source can be modified to collect your details.

### 8. Start small and keep records

Choose a shorter pack first if you're unsure, pay inside the account, and keep your invoice number and payment confirmation.

## Why lookalike sites exist

Popular names attract imitators for a simple reason: people search for a name they've heard of and click the first result that looks right. A lookalike site doesn't need to be convincing for long. It only needs one payment before the customer notices.

That's why lookalikes often:

- **Advertise in search results**, appearing above the real site for a name search.
- **Copy design and wording** from the real site, sometimes word for word.
- **Use social media and messaging** to reach people directly with "special offers".
- **Change domains frequently**, so warnings about one address don't stop the next.

Because the address changes, the most reliable defence isn't a list of bad sites. It's knowing the one good address and always going there directly.

## Protect your account on the real site

Being on the right website is the first step. Keeping your account secure is the second.

- **Use a unique password** for your Apollo Group TV account. If you reuse a password from another site and that site is breached, your account is exposed too.
- **Secure your email.** Your email account can reset your other passwords, so protect it with a strong password and two-step verification.
- **Keep your IPTV login private.** Your player login and playlist URL give access to your subscription. Don't share them outside your household or post them in screenshots. Our guide to [Xtream codes login details](/blog/xtream-codes-login) explains what each detail unlocks, and unexpected "maximum connections" errors, covered in our article on [IPTV connection limits](/blog/iptv-connection-limit), can be the first sign someone else has them.
- **Sign out of shared devices.** If you used a friend's computer or a public device to check your account, sign out afterwards.
- **Watch your notifications.** Your dashboard shows notifications about payments and your subscription. Anything you don't recognize is worth asking about.

## If you've paid a lookalike site

It happens to careful people. Acting quickly gives you the best chance of limiting the damage.

1. **Stop further payments.** Don't send more money, even if you're promised a refund in exchange for a fee.
2. **Contact your payment provider.** If you paid by card, call your bank or card issuer and explain. For payment apps, use the app's dispute or report option. Cryptocurrency payments generally can't be reversed, but report them to the exchange or app you sent from.
3. **Change passwords** if you reused your password on the fake site, starting with your email account.
4. **Report it.** In the US, the FTC accepts reports at ReportFraud.ftc.gov; other countries have their own consumer protection agencies.
5. **Tell us.** If the site impersonated Apollo Group TV, [let our support team know](/contact) with the address and any screenshots. It helps us warn other customers.

Unfortunately, we can't refund payments made to other websites, because we never received them. That's why checking the address before paying matters so much.
